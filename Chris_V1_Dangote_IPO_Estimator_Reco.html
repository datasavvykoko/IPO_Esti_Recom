    How the estimate works

    The refinery is valued with the same US$ discounted cash flow model as the accompanying spreadsheet, built from the prospectus dated 7 September 2026. Each case sets the long-run refining margin, how much crude the refinery processes, and whether the 700,000 barrel a day expansion arrives on time.

    Your first investment buys at ₦525 in the IPO, in lots of 10 shares. Later monthly or yearly investments buy at the estimated market price that month, plus 1.5% dealing costs, and any small change waits for the next purchase. Dividends are paid out to you each year, not reinvested.

    The share price follows the basis you choose. Fundamental value is what the model says the shares are worth on that date, so if the market moves there, later buyers pay less than IPO buyers. A market multiple prices the company at the EV/EBITDA multiple you set, less its debt. Today's premium assumes buyers keep paying the same mark-up over fundamental value that ₦525 represents today.

    The recommendation is a rule, not a judgement call. It weights the three cases by the odds you set, compares the odds-weighted return with the return you say you could get elsewhere, and then sizes the bet using a half-Kelly rule: the fraction of your money that maximises expected long-run growth, halved for caution. That fraction is turned into one of three answers. Above 45% it says invest it all, above 20% invest half, and below that hold off. If you are selling within two years the answer is capped at invest half. The two margin figures are measured on fundamental value, so they describe what the business must earn rather than what the market might pay. In the bear case the model floors equity value at zero, so that case is treated as a total loss.

    Return per year is money-weighted, so it accounts for when each naira went in and came out. Selling costs of 1.5% and 10% withholding tax on dividends are included. Month zero is listing, assumed December 2026.
